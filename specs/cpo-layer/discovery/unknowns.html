You are not building a CPO. You are building a studio.

The workflow you described — interview the client, draft the briefs, settle the sitemap, show directions, prototype, hand off the layout and stop before engineering — is a design studio's delivery pipeline, not a chief product officer's job. Both are real, both are buildable, and they need different documents, different gates and different people. This page separates them, hands you the vocabulary to name what you actually want, and flags eight places where this repo already has an opinion about how you build it.

A

The unknown under every other unknown

"CPO" and "design studio principal" both answer "what do we build?" — but they are measured on different things, and that difference decides every document in your layer. Read both columns before you name the layer.

Chief Product Officer

Owns one product, over years

  • Judged on outcomes — retention, activation, revenue per user. Shipping a feature is not a win.
  • The client is internal: founders, sales, support, the market.
  • Work is continuous. There is no delivery date, only a roadmap that keeps being wrong.
  • Core artifacts: product strategy, opportunity solution tree, PRDs, an OKR set, a prioritized backlog.
  • Fails by: building the right thing badly measured — no way to tell if it worked.

Design studio principal

Owns many projects, each with an end

  • Judged on deliverables accepted — the client signs off, the invoice clears, the file is handed over.
  • The client is external, pays, and often cannot describe what they want.
  • Work is bounded. Scope, rounds and sign-off gates are the whole business model.
  • Core artifacts: project brief, creative brief, sitemap, wireframes, art direction, prototype, handoff pack.
  • Fails by: infinite revisions — no gate, so "one more tweak" eats the margin.

Everything you wrote — client interviews, section briefs, sitemaps, wireframes, showing several cases to choose from, ship the layout with no build requirements — is the right column. Name the layer for what it does (a design delivery studio) and keep "CPO" for a later layer that owns the roadmap. If you insist on one layer that does both, the interview stage has to fork on day one, because "what does this client want their site to look like" and "what should we build next quarter" are not the same conversation.
